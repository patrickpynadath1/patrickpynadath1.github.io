    Showing . Pick a part on the map above to switch.

    Vocabulary: the, cat, sat, on, mat, dog.

    No vectors at all: each position is one of six symbols.

    Each token becomes a vector of length six with a single 1 at its word.

    Each word is a learned point in a vector space (two dimensions shown). Highlighted points are the five tokens.

    Masked: each token is independently hidden behind [M]. A hidden token carries no information about its word.

    GIDD: one process that mixes both kinds of discrete noise. A corrupted token is usually masked, but sometimes swapped for a random word (red).

    Uniform: each token is independently replaced by a random word (red). It looks real, so the model must work out which ones changed.

    Gaussian on one-hot vectors: noise is added to every entry. As t grows the true word’s bar sinks into the noise.

    Gaussian on embeddings: noise pushes each point off its word. The blob shows how far noise can reach at this t.

    Simplex: each token is a probability vector. Noise drifts it from its corner toward the uniform middle while it always sums to one.

    Spherical: embeddings live on the unit sphere. Noise picks a random point and moves along the arc toward it, so the state never leaves the sphere.

    Blurring: each embedding feature is smoothed along the sequence, so neighboring tokens bleed into each other. Higher features blur first.

    Two corruptions run on the same clean sequence, each in its own representation and with its own schedule. The network sees both views at once. (CANDI is masked + Gaussian.)

    Predefined schedules never change: the same curve is used from the first training step to the last.

    Semi-adaptive: the curve comes from a fixed family (here a Gumbel-shaped one, as in LangFlow) with a few parameters learned alongside the network. Press Play to watch the knobs move.

    Fully adaptive: the whole curve is refit from the training loss (CDCD-style warping, or RePlaid’s variational schedule). As the loss concentrates on some noise levels (right), the schedule warps (left) so training spends more time there.

    For masked or uniform noise it warps the keep rate.

    With two corruptions, each view has its own schedule.

    Self-conditioning off: each pass starts fresh from the noisy input.

    Self-conditioning on the expected embedding: the previous pass’s probabilities p are multiplied by the embedding table E, giving the average embedding ē the model expects. That vector is fed back in.

    Self-conditioning on the hidden state: the previous pass’s last hidden vector h is fed back directly, before it is turned into word probabilities.

    Without time conditioning the network must infer the noise level from the input itself.

    Time conditioning tells the network how noisy its input is.

    For every position, a probability over the six words for the clean token. Slide the input noise up and the bars flatten.

    Score ratios: how much more likely each word is than the current token. Not a normalized distribution.

    A plug-in estimate for uniform noise: the posterior mixed with a uniform floor that grows with noise.

    A clean-token posterior shaped by a Gaussian prior on the noisy vector, used to compute the expected clean vector.

    Only the next position is predicted, given everything to its left.

    Plain cross-entropy: predict the clean token, with every noise level counting the same. Simple, but not a likelihood bound.

    The ELBO is a bound on the data likelihood.

    For masking the ELBO is cross-entropy weighted by 1/t (as in MDLM), so nearly clean examples count much more.

    For uniform noise the ELBO has its own exact form (as in DUO).

    For GIDD the ELBO is composed from its masking and uniform parts (gidd_elbo).

    For score-ratio outputs the ELBO is score entropy (SEDD).

    For Gaussian noise, RePlaid’s three loss terms fall straight out of the ELBO. The endpoint at t = 0 gives a reconstruction cross-entropy, the endpoint at t = 1 gives a prior KL, and everything in between is an integral of a weighted MSE between the expected embedding ē and the true embedding.

    Ancestral sampling reveals tokens a few at a time in random order, one network call per step.

    Entropy-bounded sampling commits every token the model is confident about at once, so it needs fewer steps.

    A Gaussian solver (Euler, Heun, adaptive ODE or DDPM) moves every vector a little each step; all words sharpen together.

    The process’s own reverse step (simplex bridge, spherical flow or un-blurring) sharpens all positions together.

    Each view takes its own reverse step: masked tokens are revealed while the continuous view sharpens.

    SEDD's analytic step uses the score ratios to jump several masked tokens at once with exact transition probabilities.

    Discrete flow matching follows a continuous-time Markov chain: tokens jump between states, and with uniform noise a filled-in word can still change later (red).

    DDPM takes stochastic Gaussian reverse steps: every vector sharpens, with a little fresh noise added each step.

    Autoregressive decoding: one new token per step, strictly left to right.

    New tokens at each step are outlined.

    Turn on block diffusion or insertion to see how they change generation.

    Block diffusion splits the sequence into blocks. Blocks are generated left to right; inside a block, tokens are denoised in parallel, and each block can see every finished block (block-causal attention).

    Insertion lets the length change: generation starts short and new tokens are inserted between existing ones (outlined), so the model decides the length.

    Log-linear schedule: each token survives with probability α(t), which falls in a straight line from 1 to 0.

    D3PM trains on a fixed grid of discrete time steps (orange dots) along the same log-linear path.

    DUO uses the log-linear schedule plus a curriculum: early in training the uniform noise is applied to a softened relaxation of the tokens, which anneals to hard tokens.

    GIDD’s path: a token stays clean, gets swapped for a random word, or gets masked. The random-word share peaks halfway, at pu = 0.2.

    CANDI runs two schedules at once. Masking follows a log-linear path, and the Gaussian view is rank-calibrated against it.

    CADD-style hybrids also keep one schedule per view; the YAML records where its schedule alignment differs from the paper.

    CDCD-style: the base is an exponential VE schedule, and the adaptive clock refits it from the loss as training goes.

    FLM-style: time is defined by an error clock. t is the fraction of tokens a greedy argmax would get wrong, and the Gaussian σ (0.2 to 4) is calibrated to produce it.

    LangFlow-style: γ(t) follows Gumbel quantiles. Its location and scale are refit from detached cross-entropy feedback, not by the network’s optimizer. Press Play to watch them move.

    RePlaid learns its −log SNR curve with a small monotone network, trained to lower the variance of the ELBO estimate. Press Play to watch it reshape.

    The simplex process ships its own schedule, paired with a loss-derivative time sampler.

    S-FLM moves along the geodesic; the schedule sets how far along the arc the state is at time t.

    vMF spherical flows spread noise as a von Mises-Fisher distribution whose concentration falls over time.

    Blurring diffusion blurs high-frequency feature bands first and low ones last, as in the image version.

    Masked recipes only predict the masked positions; visible tokens are copied through unchanged.

    RADD drops time conditioning: the posterior depends only on which tokens are still visible.

    CANDI predicts a clean-token posterior at masked positions, fusing what the masked and Gaussian views say.

    RePlaid adds a Gaussian prior term to the network’s logits before the softmax: each word gets a bonus for how well its embedding lines up with the current noisy vector.

    D3PM’s bound is a sum over discrete steps: a reconstruction term at the start, a KL term per step, and a prior term at the end.

    The masked ELBO reduces to cross-entropy on masked tokens, weighted by 1/t under the log-linear schedule.

    SEDD trains score ratios with score entropy, using the same ELBO weighting.

    GIDD’s ELBO is assembled from its masking and uniform parts. Shapes are illustrative.
